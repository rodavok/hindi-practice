### Unit 5
- Vocabulary: the end list (pp. 60–62) with verbs as infinitives, plus body-only words: अंकल, अम्माँ (dialogue 5a), नाम, पत्र (5.2/5.3), सुनिए (Insight), the possessives उसका, उनका, किसका (5.3) and the ten short forms मुझे … किन्हें (5.5). ऋषि (a name in unit 2), खाना (“food” in unit 4) and न (the tag in unit 2) are re-listed with their unit-5 senses; दो is “two” in unit 1 and “give!” here, and no item uses both senses. कॉफ़ी follows the end list (exercise 5A.1 spells काफ़ी). The compound-verb Insight (p. 55) previews 12.4, so it has no items.
- Glosses split to keep English answers distinct: ख़त “letter” / पत्र “letter (formal word)”; जाना “to go” / चलना “to move”; कहना “to say (to)” / बताना “to tell” / बोलना “to speak”; न “don't (milder, in commands)” / मत “don't (more forceful)”; देखना “to see, look” / ढूँढ़ना “to look for, find”; किसे / किन्हें “to whom?” (singular / plural).
- Spelling: pronoun + का is written joined (उसका, उनके, किसकी), as in 5.3's उनका and dialogue 5b's उसकी; pronoun + को stays two words as in unit 4 (उस को, इन को), next to the 5.5 short forms. हमारा and तेरा are not in the ledger and are not used.
- Forms declared that are produced by the rules of this unit or earlier units: कुत्ते (`ko:7`, `tl:4`, `mn:3`), नौकरों (`ka:11`), पैसे (`tl:1`), खिलौने, कौनसी/कौनसे, the stems बोल, लिख and the command forms of the 5.1 verbs. - खान मार्केट: the Devanagari on pp. 51 and 60 is खान (no nukta), but the romanisation underlines *kh* as for ख़त. I followed the Devanagari and romanised khān; it appears only in the vocabulary.
- छूना is kept to the vocabulary: its commands (छुओ, छुइए) shorten the vowel, which the unit does not show.
- पियो (`ir:4`, `ir:7`) follows 5.4 (पानी पियो); the variant spelling पीओ is not offered anywhere.
- `ng:0`, `ng:1`, `ng:5`, `tl:2`: नहीं is explained as “the book makes commands negative with न or मत, not नहीं”. न and मत are never offered against each other (`ng:6` accepts न; मत is mentioned in the explanation as also correct).
- `ka:6`: मेरे देवर की गाड़ी — the item turns on मेरा → मेरे because देवर is oblique before की, though देवर itself does not change. The English adds “(a woman speaking)” because a देवर is a woman's relative.
- `ka:7`, `ko:3`, `ko:8`: उसका/उसे glossed “his”/“she”/“him”; the Hindi does not show the sex.
- `ka:8`: यह किसकी किताब है ? and `tl:8` accepts both यह किसका थैला है and यह थैला किसका है.
- `ka:9`: उनके for Mohan and Sita's children, with the explanation that उसके would be one person.
- `ko:2`: इन लड़कियों को समोसे खिलाइए glossed “serve these girls samosas”.
- `ko:7`, `us:3`: the को-with-specific-object explanations follow 5.4's own hedging (“often quite subtle”); check the paraphrase “much like ‘the’ in English”.
- `cd:5`: बच्चो, तुम लोग जल्दी आओ ! — vocative with तुम लोग.
- `cd:8`: रात को यह किताब पढ़ना as a deferred infinitive command (“read this book at night”).
